Gas Turbines in Industry

Gallery →

Why It Matters

Manufacturing plants are among the largest energy consumers in any industrial economy — and gas turbines offer something no utility tariff can: on-site cogeneration. By generating electricity and capturing exhaust heat for process steam or facility heating, an industrial CHP system achieves total fuel efficiency above 80%, versus 35–42% for grid power alone. For a cement plant, chemical facility, or paper mill running 24/7, this translates to millions in annual energy savings and protection against grid price volatility. Gas turbines also start and stop quickly, matching variable production schedules in ways that steam boilers cannot.

Stage 1 — Intake

Rolls-Royce Turbomeca Adour turbofan — intake and fan. (Wikimedia Commons / CC BY-SA)

01

Inlet air —
cooled for output

Industrial gas turbines draw 50–200 kg/s of air. In CHP plants, inlet air is often cooled by an absorption chiller running on the turbine's own waste heat — recovering 10–15% of output lost on hot days when industrial demand peaks.

50–200 kg/s Inlet cooling +15%
Stage 2 — Compression

Axial compressor — alternating rotor and stator blade rows. (Wikimedia Commons)

02

OPR 18:1 —
industrial grade

Industrial turbines achieve OPR 18:1, optimized for long overhaul intervals over peak efficiency. The compressor tolerates slight fouling-related degradation without shutdown — because a 1% efficiency loss is far preferable to a production outage in a 24/7 manufacturing plant.

OPR 18:1 25,000+ hr MTBO
Stage 3 — Combustion

Combustor of a sectioned Rolls-Royce Turbomeca Adour turbofan. (Wikimedia Commons / CC BY-SA)

03

DLE combustors —
<25 ppm NOx

Industrial sites operate under strict air quality permits. Dry Low Emissions (DLE) combustors keep NOx below 25 ppm — EPA Tier 4 compliant. Multi-fuel capability allows burning natural gas or process gas directly from the industrial site, cutting fuel logistics costs.

<25 ppm NOx Multi-fuel DLE
Stage 4 — Power Out

Three-stage turbine of a GE J79 engine at the MTU Museum. (Wikimedia Commons / CC BY-SA)

04

CHP output —
power + steam

The power turbine drives a generator producing on-site electricity. In CHP mode, 570°C exhaust flows into a heat recovery steam generator (HRSG), producing process steam for the plant — recovering 40%+ of fuel energy that a simple-cycle turbine discards as waste heat.

570°C exhaust → HRSG 80%+ total efficiency

Industrial CHP Configurations

Open-Cycle GT

Industrial gas turbine (open cycle) — drives a generator for on-site electricity. Simple, fast to start, suits variable manufacturing loads.

CHP System

Industrial CHP system — gas turbine generator + HRSG. Exhaust heat becomes process steam. Total fuel efficiency 80%+.
