The jet engine: a machine so powerful it could vacuum up a whole school bus of air every single second

Seriously. And that's just the beginning. Let's break down how this insane machine actually works.

How does it work? It's basically four steps. 🚀

Think of it like the world's most extreme air-powered machine.

1
Step 1: SUCK

Air enters the engine

Like breathing in, but for a 10-ton machine

At the front of every jet engine is a giant fan. It spins really fast and pulls in huge amounts of air — the same air you breathe. This air is the "fuel" that makes the whole engine go.

The fan blades are shaped like an airplane wing. That shape uses physics to push air backward, just like your hand in a swimming pool.

A Boeing 747 engine swallows about 1,000 kg of air every second — that's a small car's worth of air!
2
Step 2: SQUEEZE

Air gets crushed under pressure

Like pressing air into a tiny space until it's 40× smaller

Next, the air gets squeezed by spinning blades — like a really fast bicycle pump. Row after row of blades squeeze the air tighter and tighter.

By the end, the air is compressed to 40 times its normal pressure. That's like squishing a beach ball down to the size of a tennis ball!

The air gets so hot from being squeezed (up to 600°C) that it could melt a plastic bottle instantly.
3
Step 3: BANG

Fuel burns — and it never stops

Like a continuous explosion — one that never, ever stops

Jet fuel gets sprayed into the compressed air and — boom — it ignites! But here's the wild part: it doesn't stop. It keeps burning 24/7 the whole flight.

It's like a giant blowtorch that's always on. The temperature hits 1,500°C — that's nearly 3 times hotter than a wood fire!

The turbine blades survive 1,500°C by having tiny holes that spray cool air over them — like sweating, but for metal!
4
Step 4: BLOW

Hot air blasts out the back

The hot air escapes so fast it pushes a whole plane forward

The super-hot, super-fast gas shoots backward through turbine blades — like a pinwheel in a hurricane. Those spinning blades power the fan at the front to keep sucking in more air.

Whatever energy is left shoots out the back at hundreds of miles per hour. That's Newton's 3rd law: action (air going back) = reaction (plane going forward)! 🏃

A gas turbine power plant can go from off to full power in under 10 minutes — try that with a steam plant (takes hours)!

🤔 Why does this matter to you?

Gas turbines aren't just a cool engineering thing — they're part of your everyday life, right now:

  • Next time you fly to see family, the engine outside your window is a turbofan — doing all four steps 100 times per second.
  • The electricity keeping your school running? In many places, a gas turbine power plant is making it.
  • Gas turbines are also key to fighting climate change — they can run on hydrogen fuel in the future, producing zero CO₂.

🧠 True or False? Test yourself!

A jet engine burns fuel continuously — the fire never stops mid-flight.
✅ TRUE! Unlike a car engine (which fires in bursts), the combustor in a gas turbine has a flame that burns 100% of the time. It's lit once at startup and stays lit the whole flight.
❌ Nope! It actually burns continuously — one non-stop flame for the entire flight. That's what makes gas turbines different from car engines.
Gas turbine compressors squeeze air to around 40 times normal atmospheric pressure.
✅ TRUE! Modern high-bypass turbofans reach overall pressure ratios (OPR) of 40:1 or higher. That means the air at the compressor exit is 40× denser than the air you're breathing right now.
❌ Actually true! The OPR really does reach 40× in modern engines. That's why turbines are so powerful — super-compressed air releases way more energy when ignited.
A gas turbine power plant takes several hours to start up from cold, just like a coal plant.
❌ Nope! Gas turbines are famous for being fast starters — they can go from cold to full power in under 10 minutes. Coal and steam plants take many hours. That's why gas turbines are used for emergency peak power.
✅ CORRECT! Gas turbines are incredibly fast to start. Under 10 minutes to full power. That's one of their biggest advantages over older power plant tech.

Two types of gas turbine ✈️ ⚡

Same four steps — but used in different ways

✈️ Thrust Engine (Turbofan)
Shoots air out the back to push a plane forward. The big fan at the front is why modern jet engines are so quiet and fuel-efficient.
⚡ Power Engine (Turboshaft)
Converts all the energy into spinning power — used in helicopters, power plants, and ships. No big exhaust thrust, just pure rotation.
