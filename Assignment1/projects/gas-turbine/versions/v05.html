How a Gas Turbine Works

One of humanity's most powerful engines — explained in four stages.

Where Gas Turbines Are Used

Thrust Production

The turbine's exhaust is directed rearward at high velocity to push the vehicle forward.

✈️
Commercial Aviation
Turbofan engines power nearly every commercial aircraft — from the Boeing 737 to the Airbus A380.
🪖
Military Aircraft
Fighter jets use turbojet and afterburning turbofan engines to reach supersonic speeds.
🚀
Cruise Missiles
Small turbofan or turbojet engines propel cruise missiles over long distances at high subsonic speeds.
Power Production

The turbine shaft drives a generator, propeller, or pump instead of producing direct thrust.

⚡
Power Generation
Gas turbines produce ~20% of the world's electricity and can spin up in minutes for peak demand.
🚢
Naval Ships
Destroyers and frigates use marine gas turbines for their high power-to-weight ratio and rapid acceleration.
🚁
Helicopters
Turboshaft engines deliver shaft power to rotor systems with exceptional power-to-weight performance.
🛢️
Oil & Gas Pipelines
Gas turbines drive compressor stations that push natural gas through thousands of miles of pipeline.

Stage 01 — Intake

Air enters the engine

Atmospheric air is drawn in through the inlet at the front of the engine. A large fan accelerates massive volumes of air — a commercial jet engine can swallow over 1,000 kg of air per second.

Axial compressor animation — alternating rotor and stator blade rows. (Wikimedia Commons)

Stage 02 — Compression

Air is squeezed under pressure

Rows of rotating and fixed blades progressively compress the incoming air. Pressure can rise to 40× atmospheric — the tighter the squeeze, the more power available from the same amount of fuel.

Combustor from a sectioned Rolls-Royce Turbomeca Adour turbofan. (Wikimedia Commons / CC BY-SA)

Stage 03 — Combustion

Fuel burns continuously

Fuel is injected and ignited continuously in the combustion chamber, burning at temperatures exceeding 1,500 °C and generating a high-pressure stream of hot exhaust gas.

Three-stage turbine of a GE J79 engine at the MTU Museum. (Wikimedia Commons / CC BY-SA)

Stage 04 — Power Extraction

Hot gas spins the turbine

The high-energy exhaust blasts through turbine blades, spinning them at thousands of RPM to drive the compressor and produce useful work — electricity, propulsion, or thrust.
