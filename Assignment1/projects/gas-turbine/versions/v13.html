Engine Schematic

Intake Compression Combustion Power Out

Stage 1 — Intake

Air enters the engine

The intake duct draws atmospheric air in and smooths the flow. A large fan accelerates the air — a commercial turbofan swallows 1,000 kg/s at cruise altitude.

Sectioned Rolls-Royce Turbomeca Adour turbofan — intake and fan. (Wikimedia Commons / CC BY-SA)

Stage 2 — Compression

Pressure rises to 40× atmospheric

Alternating rotating and fixed blade rows squeeze the air in stages. Pressure reaches 40× atmospheric — the foundation of the engine's power.

Axial compressor — rotor and stator blade rows. (Wikimedia Commons)

Stage 3 — Combustion

Continuous flame at 1,500°C

Fuel burns without interruption, sustaining temperatures of 1,500°C. Hollow blades and ceramic coatings protect the turbine from the extreme heat.

Combustor of a sectioned Rolls-Royce Turbomeca Adour turbofan. (Wikimedia Commons / CC BY-SA)

Stage 4 — Power Out

Hot gas spins the turbine

Expanding exhaust blasts through turbine blades at thousands of RPM. The turbine drives the compressor and delivers thrust, shaft torque, or electrical power depending on the application.

Three-stage turbine of a GE J79 engine at the MTU Museum. (Wikimedia Commons / CC BY-SA)
