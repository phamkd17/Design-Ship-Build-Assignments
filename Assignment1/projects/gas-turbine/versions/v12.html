How a Gas Turbine Works

Tap a stage to expand it.

Stage 01
Intake
Air is drawn in and smoothed before compression
▾

Sectioned Rolls-Royce Turbomeca Adour turbofan — intake and fan. (Wikimedia Commons / CC BY-SA)

Atmospheric air is drawn in through the front inlet. The intake duct slows and smooths the airflow before the compression stage. A large fan at the front accelerates the air — a commercial turbofan swallows 1,000 kg/s at cruise altitude. The quality of the intake flow has a direct effect on how efficiently the compressor can do its job.

Stage 02
Compression
Pressure rises to 40× atmospheric in the axial compressor
▾

Axial compressor — alternating rotor and stator blade rows. (Wikimedia Commons)

The axial compressor is a series of alternating rotating blades (rotors) and fixed blades (stators). Each stage squeezes the air a little tighter. By the exit, pressure has reached 40× atmospheric. The higher the pressure ratio, the more chemical energy is unlocked from each kilogram of fuel — pressure ratio is the single biggest lever on efficiency.

Stage 03
Combustion
Fuel burns continuously at over 1,500°C
▾

Combustor of a sectioned Rolls-Royce Turbomeca Adour turbofan. (Wikimedia Commons / CC BY-SA)

Fuel is injected into the combustion chamber and ignited. Unlike a piston engine, combustion is continuous — the flame never stops. Temperatures exceed 1,500°C. Turbine blades survive this environment only through hollow cooling passages and ceramic thermal barrier coatings, among the most sophisticated materials in engineering.

Stage 04
Power Out
Exhaust spins the turbine and delivers useful work
▾

Three-stage turbine of a GE J79 engine at the MTU Museum. (Wikimedia Commons / CC BY-SA)

The high-energy exhaust blasts through turbine blades at thousands of RPM. The turbine drives the compressor via a shared shaft — keeping the cycle self-sustaining. Depending on the application, remaining energy becomes raw thrust out the back of a jet, shaft torque for a helicopter rotor or ship propeller, or electricity from a coupled generator.
