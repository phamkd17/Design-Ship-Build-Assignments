Gas Turbine Thermodynamics:
The Brayton Cycle

Ideal cycle analysis, real-cycle deviations, stage-level performance parameters, and current research frontiers.

1. The Brayton Cycle: Ideal and Real

The ideal Brayton cycle consists of three internally reversible processes: isentropic compression (1→2s), constant-pressure heat addition (2→3), and isentropic expansion (3→4s). In the ideal cycle, all compression and expansion work are perfectly recovered and no pressure losses occur in the combustor or ducts.

Real cycles deviate significantly. Compression and expansion are polytropic (irreversible), the combustor introduces stagnation pressure losses (typically 3–5% of inlet total pressure), and heat transfer losses occur across all components. These deviations are captured through component efficiencies and pressure recovery factors.

Ideal Cycle Thermal Efficiency

Thermal efficiency (ideal, simple-cycle)
ηth = 1 − (T₁ / T₂) = 1 − OPR−(γ−1)/γ
γ ≈ 1.4 (air, cold), γ ≈ 1.33 (combustion products). Efficiency is independent of TIT in the ideal cycle; TIT sets specific work output.

2. Key Performance Parameters

Overall pressure ratio
OPR = P₃ / P₁
Modern high-bypass turbofans: OPR 40–60. Industrial gas turbines: OPR 18–35.
Isentropic efficiency — compressor
ηc = (T2s − T₁) / (T₂ − T₁)
T₂s = isentropic exit temperature. Typical ηc: 0.85–0.90 for multi-stage axial compressors.
Isentropic efficiency — turbine
ηt = (T₃ − T₄) / (T₃ − T4s)
Typical ηt: 0.88–0.92 for HPT with blade cooling accounted for.
Net specific work output
Wnet = cp [(T₃ − T₄) − (T₂ − T₁)]
cp ≈ 1.005 kJ/(kg·K) for air. Turbine inlet temperature (TIT / TET) is the primary driver of specific work; increasing TIT by 30 K yields roughly 1% more specific work.
Specific fuel consumption (SFC)
SFC = ṁf / F = f / (Ve − V0)
f = fuel-air ratio (≈0.025–0.035 at stoichiometry). SFC improvements drive the majority of engine development cost.

3. Stage-by-Stage Analysis

Stage 1 — Intake / Inlet

Ram pressure recovery and inlet total pressure ratio

The inlet must decelerate the incoming flow from flight Mach number to approximately Mach 0.5–0.6 at the compressor face with minimal total pressure loss. For subsonic inlets, ram pressure recovery ηr = Pt,2/Pt,0 approaches 0.97–0.995. Supersonic inlets (mixed compression or external compression) introduce oblique shocks; MIL-SPEC recovery: ηr = 1 − 0.075(M₀ − 1)^1.35 for M₀ < 5.

Inlet total pressure distortion (circumferential and radial) can trigger compressor stall; distortion tolerance is characterized by the inlet distortion descriptor DC60 or AIP survey data.

Stage 2 — Compressor

Stage loading, degree of reaction, surge margin

Stage work coefficient: ψ = ΔH₀ / U² = (Vθ2 − Vθ1) / U. Typical ψ: 0.3–0.45. Degree of reaction: R = 1 − (Vθ1 + Vθ2) / 2U ≈ 0.5 for symmetric stages. Polytropic efficiency accounts for stage-stacking: ηp,c = (γ−1)/γ × ln(OPR) / ln(T₂/T₁).

Surge margin: SM = (PRsurge − PRop) / PRop × (ṁop / ṁsurge). Minimum SM requirement typically 15–20%. Active surge control (bleed valves, variable stator vanes) extends the stable operating range. Online compressor washing reduces fouling-induced PR degradation; offline washing with detergent restores ≈1% efficiency annually.

Borescope inspection: typical interval 4,000–8,000 equivalent operating hours (EOH) per OEM schedule.

Stage 3 — Combustor

Combustion efficiency, pattern factor, NOx formation

Combustion efficiency ηb ≥ 0.9995 across the operating envelope. Pattern factor: PF = (Tmax,exit − Tmean,exit) / (Tmean,exit − Tinlet). Target PF < 0.25 to protect HPT first-stage blades from hot streaks.

Thermal NOx follows the Zeldovich mechanism: N₂ + O → NO + N, N + O₂ → NO + O. NOx production is exponentially sensitive to flame temperature; lean premixed prevaporized (LPP) combustors and rich-burn/quick-quench/lean-burn (RQL) architectures reduce EINOx by 50–80% vs. conventional diffusion flames. Idle CO and UHC emissions constrain minimum flame temperatures at low power.

Stage 4 — Turbine

Blade cooling, creep life, tip clearance, TBC condition

HPT blades operate at gas temperatures exceeding Tgas = 1,800 K with substrate alloy (single-crystal Ni superalloy, e.g., CMSX-4) safe limit around Twall = 1,150 K. Cooling effectiveness: θ = (Tgas − Twall) / (Tgas − Tcoolant); target θ ≈ 0.55–0.65. Cooling air penalty: every 1% of compressor delivery air used for cooling reduces cycle efficiency by ~0.3%.

Larson–Miller parameter governs creep life: LMP = T(log tr + C). Tip clearance management (active clearance control, ACC) using thermal actuation of casing reduces steady-state clearance to 0.2–0.4% of blade span, improving turbine efficiency by 0.5–1%.

TBC (7 wt% Y₂O₃ stabilised ZrO₂, 100–300 μm EB-PVD) reduces metal temperature by 100–200 K and extends hot-section life 2–3×.

4. Current Research Frontiers

Ceramic Matrix Composites (CMC)

SiC/SiC CMC components (combustor liners, HPT shrouds, nozzle guide vanes) reduce component mass by 30–40% vs. Ni superalloys and permit 100–200 K higher operating temperatures. GE Aviation CFM LEAP and GE9X production engines incorporate CMC combustor liners and HPT stage-1 shrouds. Primary challenges: environmental barrier coatings (EBC) for water vapour recession, interlaminar shear strength, and NDE inspection methodologies.

Additive Manufacturing for Cooling Channels

Laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) and directed energy deposition (DED) enable conformal cooling channel geometries impossible with conventional investment casting. Lattice structures and micro-channel networks reduce cooling air demand by 10–20%, directly improving cycle efficiency. Current OEM certification barriers: anisotropic fatigue properties, surface finish Ra, and powder traceability.

Hydrogen Combustion

H₂ LHV: 120 MJ/kg (vs. 43 MJ/kg for Jet-A). Key challenges: (1) NOx — H₂/air adiabatic flame temperature ≈ 2,480 K vs. 2,230 K for CH₄/air; Zeldovich NOx increases exponentially; lean premixed ultra-low-NOx burner architectures required. (2) Flashback — H₂ laminar flame speed ≈ 3.2 m/s vs. 0.4 m/s for CH₄; autoignition delay times orders of magnitude shorter. (3) Embrittlement of high-strength steels in fuel system components. Siemens, Mitsubishi, and GE have demonstrated 100% H₂ combustion at laboratory scale; phased blending (20–30% H₂ by volume) is near-term commercial pathway.

Propulsive efficiency: ηp = 2V₀ / (Ve + V₀). For fixed thrust, increasing BPR lowers Ve toward V₀, raising ηp asymptotically. This is the primary thermodynamic driver for ultra-high-bypass ratio (UHBR) and open-rotor architectures.
