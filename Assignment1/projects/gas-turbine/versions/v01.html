How a Gas Turbine Works

A gas turbine is an engine that converts the energy in burning fuel into useful mechanical work or electricity.

The Four Stages

1. Intake

Air is drawn into the engine at the front.

2. Compression

The compressor squeezes the air, raising its pressure and temperature. Compressed air holds more energy potential.

3. Combustion

Fuel is injected into the compressed air and ignited. The burning mixture expands rapidly, producing hot, high-pressure gas.

4. Exhaust (Power Extraction)

The hot gas rushes through the turbine blades, spinning them at high speed. This spin drives the compressor and produces useful work — electricity in a power plant, or thrust in a jet engine.

Why It Matters

Gas turbines power commercial aircraft, naval ships, and about 20% of the world's electricity. They are valued for their high power-to-weight ratio and ability to start quickly.
