How a Gas Turbine Works

A gas turbine turns the chemical energy of burning fuel into mechanical power — in four continuous stages.

1 Intake

Fresh air is drawn into the engine through the inlet. The large fan at the front accelerates a massive volume of air into the compressor section every second.

2 Compression

Rows of rotating blades squeeze the incoming air, raising its pressure up to 40× atmospheric. Higher pressure means more energy available for combustion.

3 Combustion

Fuel (jet fuel or natural gas) is injected into the compressed air and ignited. The mixture burns continuously, producing a high-temperature, high-pressure stream of gas.

4 Power Extraction

Hot gas blasts through turbine blades, spinning them at thousands of RPM. This rotation drives the compressor (and a generator or propeller), producing useful power.
