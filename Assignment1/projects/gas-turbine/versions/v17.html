Gas Turbines in Oil & Gas

Why It Matters

The oil and gas industry depends on gas turbines for everything from pipeline compression to offshore power generation. No other driver technology combines the power density, fuel flexibility, and remote-site reliability that the industry demands. A compressor drive station on the Trans-Alaska Pipeline uses gas turbines running on the natural gas it pumps — eliminating fuel logistics. Offshore platforms use turbines because their weight per megawatt is a fraction of diesel alternatives, and every tonne saved offshore lowers platform cost significantly. These machines run for 25,000+ hours between major overhauls, often in remote locations where maintenance access is measured in helicopter flights, not road trips.

Oil & Gas Applications

🛢️
Pipeline Compression
Drive compressor stations up to 50 MW. Trans-Alaska, Nord Stream, TAPI.
🏭
Offshore Platforms
Compact power generation — weight per MW critical for platform economics.
⛽
Wellhead Power
Remote site generation running on field gas — no external fuel required.
🌡️
LNG Processing
Drive refrigeration compressors for liquefaction trains. Up to 120 MW.

Two Pipeline Drive Configurations

Turbine Driver

Industrial gas turbine — 100% shaft output drives pipeline compressor. No generator, no bypass, maximum mechanical power.

Pipeline Compressor

Centrifugal pipeline compressor — gas turbine shaft pushes natural gas at 700–1,000 psi. One driver, up to 50 MW, 100–500 MMscfd throughput.
