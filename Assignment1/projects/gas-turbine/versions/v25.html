Product Knowledge

How it works — in plain language

01

Air In

A large fan draws in massive volumes of air. This is the raw material the engine turns into power.

02

Compress

Spinning blades squeeze the air to 40 times atmospheric pressure. Compressed air holds far more energy when ignited.

03

Ignite

Fuel is injected and burns continuously at 1,500°C. This sustained combustion is the engine's power source.

04

Power Out

Hot gas spins turbine blades. That rotation becomes electricity, thrust, or shaft torque — depending on the application.

Objection Handling

Ready answers to common questions

Aren't gas turbines expensive?
Capital cost is higher than diesel generators, but total cost of ownership tells a different story. Gas turbines have 40–60% thermal efficiency vs. 30–35% for diesel at scale, directly lowering fuel spend over the asset's 25+ year life. Higher uptime (MTBO of 25,000–50,000 equivalent hours) means fewer outages and less revenue lost. For any application running more than 4,000 hours per year, the economics consistently favour gas turbines.
What about renewables replacing gas turbines?
Gas turbines are the backbone of the energy transition, not its victim. Renewable energy is intermittent — the sun doesn't always shine and the wind doesn't always blow. Every gigawatt of solar or wind installed increases demand for fast-response firm capacity to balance the grid. Gas turbines are the only proven technology that can fulfil this role today. And as hydrogen becomes available, gas turbines can run on it directly — making them a zero-carbon asset in the future grid.
How does it compare to diesel generators?
For small, intermittent loads under 5 MW, diesel wins on capital cost. But at scale, the comparison reverses decisively: gas turbines offer 30–40% lower fuel cost per MWh, 50–70% lower CO₂ emissions, higher availability, and a fraction of the maintenance labour hours per MWh generated. For industrial facilities, data centres, and grid-scale applications, gas turbines are the right tool.
How long does it take to install and commission?
Aero-derivative gas turbines in modular containerized packages can be installed and commissioned in 12–18 months — significantly faster than combined-cycle steam plants (36–48 months) or nuclear (10+ years). Simple-cycle peaking units can be on contract-to-power in under 12 months for urgent grid needs. Speed to market is one of the strongest selling points in today's constrained grid environment.

Application Reference

Thrust applications vs. Power applications

✈️ Thrust Applications — Turbofan

Designed to produce forward thrust. A large fan pushes air around the core for fuel efficiency. Powers all commercial aviation and most military aircraft. Customer value: unmatched fuel efficiency at cruise, low emissions, long TBO.

⚡ Power Applications — Turboshaft

All energy extracted as shaft power — no net thrust from exhaust. Drives generators, helicopter rotors, ship propellers, and pipeline compressors. Customer value: highest power density for shaft-drive applications, fast start, fuel flexibility.
